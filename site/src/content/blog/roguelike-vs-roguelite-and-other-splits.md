The [first glossary post](/blog/indie-genre-jargon-decoded/) covered what each label on the index
means on its own. A few of those labels only make sense in contrast to a near-neighbor that looks
similar from the outside but plays differently. Three worth splitting apart.

## Roguelike vs. roguelite

"Roguelike" gets used loosely enough now that it mostly just signals "runs that end and restart."
The stricter, older sense means something closer to the original *Rogue*: permadeath, procedural
levels, turn-based or grid-based play, and systems complex enough that no two runs solve the same
way. "Roguelite" is the looser modern branch — it keeps permadeath and procedural structure but
adds something a strict roguelike wouldn't: meta-progression that survives a death, unlocks that
carry between runs, sometimes real-time action instead of turns.

Most games marketed as "roguelike" today are, by that stricter definition, roguelites. The index
tags [Sovereign Tower](https://sovereign-tower.hunt-games.com/) with the broader, popularly
understood label rather than litigating which side of that line it falls on — the useful thing to
know going in is that "roguelike" here means runs with real, remembered consequences, not
necessarily a return to *Rogue*'s specific rule set.

## Simulation vs. sandbox

Both describe systems with real inputs and outputs you're meant to learn, and the index uses both
labels — but they answer different questions. A simulation has a goal already built in: a recipe to
get right, a wave to survive, a batch to optimize. You're measured against something.
[Schedule 1](https://schedule1.hunt-games.com/) and [Iron Nest](https://ironnest.hunt-games.com/)
are both this — mix calculators and wave math exist because there's a correct-ish answer to find.

A sandbox hands you the same kind of interlocking systems but no built-in goal — you decide what
you're optimizing for. [Sandustry](https://sandustry.hunt-games.com/) is the network's sandbox
entry: nothing tells you what to automate next, which is also why its label on the index is
"Sandbox / Factory" rather than plain "Simulation."

## Cozy sim vs. farming sim

Every farming sim is a cozy sim, but not every cozy sim is a farming sim, and the difference is
whether there's a production loop at the center of it. [Doloc Town](https://doloctown.hunt-games.com/)
is cozy without that loop — the town runs on routines, relationships, and seasons, but there's
nothing you're growing or harvesting on a schedule. [Fields of Mistria](https://mistria.hunt-games.com/)
adds that loop back in: crops, seasons, and townsfolk, but with planting and harvest timing as the
thing the whole game is actually organized around.
